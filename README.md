## Overview

Curvature Polarization Transport Gravity (CPTG) is a geometric-gravity framework in which ordinary physical stress-energy remains the material source while curvature polarization and curvature transport provide additional geometric response.

- **Curvature polarization** describes how sourced geometry reorganizes according to field strength and structural state.
- **Curvature transport** describes how that organized response is retained, redistributed, or directionally carried through an extended or evolving system.

CPTG is a framework of scale-specific reductions rather than one reduced equation applied unchanged to every physical regime. The current published program spans resolved galaxy dynamics, pressure-supported clusters, Structural Mode `N`, dissociative cluster-merger lensing, finite compact objects and black holes, the native geometric π branch and its observational projections, CMB and BAO comparison layers, the Hubble and horizon sectors, and a universal geometric nuclear-reaction construction.

CPTG is not a tunable halo-component model. In the framework, the additional response attributed to CPTG remains geometric; the material source remains ordinary physical stress-energy. The public record separates theory definitions, conditioned reconstructions, held-out or external comparisons, reproducible benchmarks, and active software development.

## Gravity and Structure

### Galaxies and pressure-supported clusters

The current resolved-gravity program uses the same registered CPTG source-response architecture in two different support geometries: rotation-supported SPARC galaxies and pressure-supported galaxy clusters.

For SPARC galaxies, the v1.13.0 workbench reads the measured radius, observed rotation speed and uncertainty, and tabulated gas, disk, and bulge contributions. The current CPTG reconstruction retains the stellar templates at their reference normalization and does not add a fitted disk or bulge mass-to-light rescaling.

The workbench supports single-object and batch analysis, displays observed/baryonic/CPTG curves, reports the recovered reduced-state coordinates, and evaluates Structural Mode only after the gravitational solution is complete. Catalog morphology is not used to determine the field solution.

For pressure-supported clusters, the same registered field architecture is evaluated through the spherical support reconstruction appropriate to the system. X-COP is the primary cluster reconstruction; ACCEPT provides an independent support-reconstruction sensitivity comparison where used.

[Download CPTG SPARC Browser Workbench v1.13.0](https://github.com/CLG2025/CPTG/releases/tag/v1.13.0)

[SPARC data source](https://astroweb.case.edu/SPARC/): Lelli, McGaugh, and Schombert, *The Astronomical Journal* **152**, 157 (2016).

### Structural Mode N

**[Structural Mode N](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23172423)** is a dimensionless post-solution measure of gravitational organization derived from the solved CPTG field. It is not a fit parameter, acceleration scale, morphology input, or branch selector.

Its general form is:

```text
N = R / λ
```

where `R` is the outer structural radius of the declared realization and `λ` is the geometry-appropriate organization length derived from the solved field.

Each independently reconstructed field has its own Structural Mode fingerprint. Independent `N` values are reported separately rather than averaged. The parent meaning is common across regimes, while the organization functional follows the geometry of the solved object. CSMI remains a downstream galaxy-classification layer built from the solved continuous mode.

<details>
<summary><strong>What Structural Mode asks</strong></summary>

<br>

> Where are the important structural changes concentrated inside this solved gravitational field?

Lower `N` places the dominant structural organization farther outward; higher `N` places it farther inward. The exact geometry-specific construction is given in the current Structural Mode paper.

</details>

The outer-slope convergence test extends the solved galaxy field beyond the last measured SPARC radius and asks whether the reduced CPTG solution approaches its predicted far-outer trend rather than drifting arbitrarily. It is a continuation test of the solved equation, not a fit to artificial outer data.

### Compact objects and black holes

The current compact-object construction uses a finite physical interior and requires matching to the ordinary Schwarzschild/Kerr exterior where the additional CPTG response becomes negligible. Horizons are allowed; an infinite singularity is not treated as a completed physical state.

The compact reduction separates localized source mass from the geometric contribution to the conserved exterior gravitational charge. The present rotating quantities, including compact Structural Mode, are evaluated downstream of the closed compact state rather than being promoted to a solved general axisymmetric interior.

See **[Curvature-Bounded Black Holes and Compact Objects in CPTG](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23172326)** and the **[GWTC-5 compact-population paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23172497)**.

## Cosmology and Comparison Coordinates

CPTG cosmology begins with one native geometric π branch. Acoustic, affine-luminosity, CMB, BAO, lensing, and distance quantities are comparison projections or downstream coordinates of that native geometry rather than independent cosmologies.

### Native, acoustic, and affine-luminosity coordinates

```text
p_C = π
p_ac = 3 - π/100 = 2.968584073464
G_T = p_ac / π = 0.944929658551
sqrt(G_T) = 0.972074924351

G_L^aff = 10/9
sqrt(G_L^aff) = 1.054092553389

H0^(π)      = 69.4162507897 km s^-1 Mpc^-1
H0_ac^CPTG  = 67.4777967351 km s^-1 Mpc^-1
H0_L^aff    = 73.1711530416 km s^-1 Mpc^-1
```

The acoustic and affine-luminosity coordinates are fixed projections of the same native history. The luminosity normalization `10/9` is derived internally; SH0ES is used afterward as an external observational comparison rather than as the input that defines the branch.

### Hubble tension

The **[Hubble-tension paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23172759)** treats the CMB/acoustic and local luminosity determinations as different observational projections of one native CPTG history:

```text
67.4778  <-  69.4163  ->  73.1712
acoustic      native       luminosity
```

The acoustic projection is compared with Planck, while the affine-luminosity projection is compared independently with SH0ES. The point of the construction is not to average the two observed endpoints into a compromise value, but to identify their distinct comparison coordinates relative to the same native phase.

### Cosmic standard ruler

The **[CPTG Cosmic Standard Ruler](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22935182)** carries the acoustic projection from the CMB into DESI BAO comparison coordinates. The analysis uses the CPTG acoustic ruler and DESI BAO ratios without promoting the conventional `r_drag` coordinate to a CPTG-native input.

### Cosmological horizon

The **[CPTG horizon paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23172701)** treats the horizon problem through native geometric containment and common-phase synchronization rather than by introducing a separate inflationary scalar-field mechanism into CPTG.

### Commissioning and mass-seven transport

The **[commissioning paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22425309)** established the first complete CPTG source-to-reaction-to-rate-to-network chain through `D(p,γ)³He` and the primordial mass-seven transport problem. The current primary-network implementation separately validates the locked live mass-seven transport inside PRIMAT; all `7/7` paired coordinates pass, with a worst relative survival disagreement of `0.018083%` from the locked target and negligible non-mass-seven control shifts.

The computational companion separates native network authority from the reduced continuation:

The external `Y_A` values beyond the native network boundary are prescribed-bath reduced-transport populations. They are not presented as self-consistent primordial heavy-element abundances from a coupled PRIMAT heavy-nucleus network.

## Reproducing CPTG Results

The repository contains both historical reduced-limit benchmarks and newer paper-specific reconstruction/evidence packages. Reproduce a result with the method and package attached to that result rather than assuming one script represents every later CPTG reduction.

### Recommended workflow

1. Identify the paper or benchmark being reproduced.
2. Use the corresponding release, reconstruction package, or repository directory named by that work.
3. Preserve the declared input data, coordinate definitions, and fixed theory settings.
4. Run the documented scripts locally with the required Python/runtime environment.
5. Compare the generated outputs with the paper tables, figures, residuals, and included evidence records.

The compact `CPTG_academic_package.zip` remains the starting point for the original reduced-limit SPARC and Bullet Cluster benchmarks. Current v1.13.0 galaxy calculations, cross-regime cluster/compact reconstructions, cosmology comparisons, and nuclear-network validations use their corresponding current paper/release materials.

The individual publications provide the detailed evidence manifests, integrity information, commands, raw outputs, and scope limitations where those records are part of the published validation chain.

## Relation to MOND and ΛCDM

CPTG is tested against observations that are also commonly discussed through MOND-type phenomenology or ΛCDM halo-sector modeling, but the constructions are not equivalent.

- **MOND-style comparisons** modify the relation between baryonic acceleration and the inferred gravitational response through a low-acceleration law.
- **ΛCDM analyses** commonly attribute the additional galaxy- and cluster-scale gravitational response to non-baryonic matter components embedded in a cosmological model.
- **CPTG** keeps ordinary physical sources on the material side and attributes the additional response to curvature polarization and curvature transport.

The current v1.13.0 SPARC result is presented as a CPTG support-conditioned reconstruction. Comparison models and older reduced benchmarks remain useful as external reference points, but they do not set the acceptance condition for the current cross-regime CPTG law.
